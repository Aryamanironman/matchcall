The validator accepts 95% of lines, but acceptance means “passes the grounding checks,” not “is correct.” The template is shorter and safer, yet its team attribution fails in the same record, which points to the shared feature layer as well as the language model.

Comparison 2

Live vs pre-recorded

The full-context run gets both goals right. A 5-minute causal chunk is faster to first line, but it attributes both goals to the home side and invents an earlier goal. The current system is pre-recorded only; chunking alone does not make it live.

Ground truth from the score-bug reader: the two anchors in the chunk are away 44:33 and away 45:42; the broadcast score flips 2–1 to 2–2. The chunk's generated lines still say Argentina. The side is lost between the anchor and the realised line, and the event classifier also promotes an earlier passage to a goal. This is why “faster” is not the same as “live.”

YOLO26n is fractionally ahead, but the runs are not equal-budget: 50 vs 33 epochs, 2.39 M vs 53 M parameters. Per-class YOLO26n mAP50–95: Player 0.621, Ball 0.291, Referee 0.530. The low Ball score is why the ball path is interpolated rather than trusted.

Verdict. The score-bug goal path is reliable enough to build on. The general event classifier is not: it finds one third of reference shots, no tackles, and produces almost twice as many pass/dead-time lines as event lines. Report goals and object detection separately; do not turn detection mAP into an event-spotting claim.

Cost

Discussion

What the numbers support

The honest claim is narrower than “AI commentary.” It is a cheap, auditable pre-recorded pipeline whose goals are reliable and whose general event layer is not yet good enough for broadcast output.

HOLDS
Goals2/2, precision and recall 1.00 via the score bug.
Cost$0.39 cloud, $0.01 local per match.
AuditabilityFacts, validator and ClearML runs are inspectable.
End-to-end64.5 min in, 64.5 min voiced out, 43/43 lines.
DOES NOT HOLD
Live16.6 min to first line; chunking is faster but wrong.
Events0.40 overall recall; no tackles; two of six shots.
TeamsWrong side survives both AI and template paths.
LanguageInvented detail, repetition and malformed names.
WHY
Score bugHigh precision, but the side must be carried into the line.
ClassifierResNet windows over-produce pass/dead time; rare events are missed.
ChunkingShort context loses the match story and makes false goals easier.
ValidatorChecks words, not truth; it accepts 95% of lines.

Paper claim. The pipeline is a working, low-cost, auditable pre-recorded commentary system. Its strongest contribution is the separation of deterministic facts from language and the score-bug goal path; its weakest links are general event spotting, team-side propagation and live latency. The paper should report those weaknesses as findings, not hide them.
